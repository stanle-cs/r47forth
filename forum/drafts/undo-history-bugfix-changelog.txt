[b]Changelog[/b]
[list]
[*]Unlabeled value rows showed a lone -, easy to read as a minus sign next to a negative number. The placeholder is now (val).
[*]ENTER did nothing in the history view on the real keyboard (the key never reached the view). It restores the selected level now.
[*]Restoring a level with ENTER left no (now) entry, so there was no way back to where you jumped from. REDO returns there now.
[*]ENTER on an empty history inserted an ENTER level, choosing a second level rewrote the list, and (now) never appeared. One cause for all three (the keypress was saved as an operation before the view handled it), fixed together.
[*]Keys with no meaning in the view (digits, shifted functions) acted on the calculator underneath, which could end in a firmware error. They are ignored while the view is open.
[*]f-UP could open the view in the middle of a command prompt. It waits until the prompt is done.
[*]After an undo, the next operation left a hole in the level numbers (rows like 05 03 02 01). Numbering stays consecutive now.
[*]UNDO across a ~ could land on the wrong level, and the next press could walk forward. It lands on the newest stored level now, further presses keep going back, and REDO returns.
[*]Two equal states on either side of a ~ were folded into one level, so an undo skipped a step and the mark sat on the wrong row. They stay separate levels.
[*]After undoing back past a skipped state, the next stored level could get a ~ for a gap that was not there. It does not appear anymore.
[*]Choosing a level while memory was too tight to restore it could push that same level out of the history. The restore refuses first, with the level intact.
[*]With memory full, a restore that failed halfway could leave the stock one-step undo half overwritten, and pressing UNDO gave a state that never existed. A failed restore leaves everything untouched, or turns off the one-step undo when it cannot (UNDO walks the history instead). Freeing memory and trying again works.
[/list]
