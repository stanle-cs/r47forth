This is a package that draws maths on R47 the way it looks on paper, and keeps the calculation that made a number. Stock R47 puts a fraction on one line with small raised and lowered digits, and a root sign has no bar over it. Press + and you get the answer, the calculator has already forgotten what you asked. Here a fraction gets a real bar with the numerator on top, roots get their bar, and the working stays around for you to look at.

Natural display is on when you install it. The formula line above the stack is off, turn it on with PTLIN.

[list]
[*]PSHOW draws the X value on the whole screen, biggest font that fits.
[*]PHIST opens the formula browser. UP and DOWN move the selection, ENTER puts the selected result back in X, EXIT leave. A row wider than the screen pans with the .d key.
[*]PCLR empties the formula history.
[*]EQSHW draws the current equation in 2D on the whole screen (it sits on the EQN menu too).
[*]PPON toggles natural display, PTLIN toggles the formula line on T.
[*]All six are on the PP menu under DISP, and in the catalog, and they can be assigned to keys.
[*]Both toggles are system flags, PPRTY and PTLINE, so SF and CF in SYSFL work on them.
[/list]

[b]When it refuses[/b]

If anything about a value is unusual the package draws nothing and the normal line renders. Strings, matrices, dates and long integers never get the 2D treatment. A formula too tall for the room it has is dropped. Why refuse instead of drawing something close? A picture of a fraction that isn't the number in X is worse than the plain line, and you can't tell by looking. The same rule covers the formula memory. When the package can't say honestly how a number was made, it keeps the number and drops the description.

You'll probably never see it refuse on ordinary arithmetic. It shows up on the awkward stuff (a matrix has no shape a bar and a numerator can describe).

Numbers are formatted by the calculator first and rearranged after. Display mode, rounding and digit grouping come out exactly as they did before.

[b]Equations[/b]

The equation parser gains SUM, PROD, INTEG and DERIV. Semicolons go between the arguments (comma is already the thousands separator in some formats).

[code]SUM(body;var;from;to)
PROD(body;var;from;to)
INTEG(body;var;from;to)
DERIV(body;var;at)[/code]
SUM and PROD take an optional step. DERIV takes an optional order for the second derivative. They nest.
[attachment=3]pp-attach-3-nesting.png[/attachment]

That's an integral of a second derivative over a sum and a product, drawn by EQSHW. It's the biggest thing the renderer will take, and the test suite keeps it as the capacity case.

[b]Limits[/b]
[list]
[*]The formula memory is session-only, and it follows the keys you press. Programs, the solver and the integrator don't add to it.
[*]About a dozen formulas are kept. A long enough chain runs out of room and comes back as a plain value.
[*]A typed number over 30 characters drops the formula rather than show part of it.
[*]The full-screen PHIST view can't pan, so a formula too wide for it is left out. The browser pans.
[*]Upstream's second derivative sizes its step from x, and for x at 0.005 or below the answer is wrong (x=0 and x from 0.01 up are fine). Set the d_d variable to fix it, the delta softkey in the derivative menu. That one isn't the package.
[/list]

[b]Install[/b]

Unzip the attachment into packages/pretty-print and build with the package manager [url]https://forum.swissmicros.com/viewtopic.php?f=46&t=4876[/url]:
[code]make sim CUSTOM_PKG=packages/pretty-print[/code]
It doesn't need the other packages, and it composes with both if you run them:
[code]make sim CUSTOM_PKG=packages/forth-core,packages/undo-history,packages/pretty-print[/code]
For the calculator it's make dmcp5r47 with the same CUSTOM_PKG. The firmware grows by 25 KB. The working memory is all static, nothing comes out of the pool.
Package base is r47forth commit 70f8b7db7. Upstream's full test suite runs green with the package active, alone and combined with the other two, and my own tests add about a hundred cases on top. Everything above is from the simulator, I haven't flashed a real DM42n with this yet. So make a calculator backup and flash at your own risk.
